<blockquote>If AI is replacing software engineers, who designs and deploys the systems?<strong>Process engineers do.</strong></blockquote>
<div class="pillars">
<div><h4>The wall used to be code</h4><p>Reinforcement learning, digital twins and AI agents fit chemical engineering problems well. Software ecosystems kept them out of the curriculum and out of the plant.</p></div>
<div><h4>The wall is gone</h4><p>AI coding assistants now turn a process described in plain English into working code. The bottleneck has moved from writing code to judging it.</p></div>
<div><h4>Judgment is an engineering skill</h4><p>Breaking a problem down, testing each piece and checking it against a balance is what engineers already do. These courses make that the method.</p></div>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<span class="num">02 · The method</span>
<h2>Every course runs the same four moves</h2>
<p>Learners meet a live process before any equation, co-write the code with an AI assistant, let physics and tests decide whether it is right, and finish with a tool other people can use.</p>
</div>

<div class="tile"><div class="ic">C</div><h4>Concept · hour 1</h4><p>One idea, explained with a process engineers already know: a fed-batch reactor, a CSTR, a plant upset.</p></div>
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">S</div><h4>Studio · hour 2</h4><p>Live vibe coding. The class breaks the problem down and builds it piece by piece with an AI assistant.</p></div>
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">L</div><h4>Lab · hour 3</h4><p>Hands-on build and test. Every lab ends with something that runs and a check that proves it.</p></div>

<div class="section-head">
<span class="num">04 · Why this series</span>
<h2>What makes it different</h2>
</div>
<div class="grid-3">
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">1</div><h4>No setup wall</h4><p>A browser and a free AI assistant account. No installations, no terminal, no environment debugging.</p></div>
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">2</div><h4>Chemical engineering first</h4><p>Fed-batch fermentation, CSTRs, oxygen transfer, SOPs and batch records. Never toy datasets for their own sake.</p></div>
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">3</div><h4>Verify before you trust</h4><p>Every model, policy and agent is checked against balances, constraints and tests before anyone relies on it.</p></div>
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">4</div><h4>Idea to deployment</h4><p>Each course ends with a shareable app or service, not a notebook that only runs on one laptop.</p></div>
<div class="tile"><div class="ic">5</div><h4>Any AI platform</h4><p>Taught with Claude by default; the method transfers to ChatGPT, Gemini, GitHub Copilot or Cursor.</p></div>
