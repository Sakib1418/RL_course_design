If AI is replacing software engineers, who designs and deploys the systems?Process engineers do.

The wall used to be code

Reinforcement learning, digital twins and AI agents fit chemical engineering problems well. Software ecosystems kept them out of the curriculum and out of the plant.

The wall is gone

AI coding assistants now turn a process described in plain English into working code. The bottleneck has moved from writing code to judging it.

Judgment is an engineering skill

Breaking a problem down, testing each piece and checking it against a balance is what engineers already do. These courses make that the method.

02 · The method

Every course runs the same four moves

Learners meet a live process before any equation, co-write the code with an AI assistant, let physics and tests decide whether it is right, and finish with a tool other people can use.

C

Concept · hour 1

One idea, explained with a process engineers already know: a fed-batch reactor, a CSTR, a plant upset.

S

Studio · hour 2

Live vibe coding. The class breaks the problem down and builds it piece by piece with an AI assistant.

L

Lab · hour 3

Hands-on build and test. Every lab ends with something that runs and a check that proves it.

03 · Who it is for

Designed for engineers, not programmers

No coding, command line or version control is assumed. Everything runs in a web browser; Git appears once, gently, in the final week.

U

Undergraduates

Juniors and seniors who want AI on their transcript and a working project to show for it.

G

Graduate students

Researchers who need a simulator, a controller or an agent for their own process, quickly.

P

Practicing engineers

Process, controls and manufacturing engineers who want to prototype AI tools on their own units.

L

Leaders and managers

Technical leaders who must judge AI proposals, vendors and pilots without an AI background.

04 · Why this series

What makes it different

1

No setup wall

A browser and a free AI assistant account. No installations, no terminal, no environment debugging.

2

Chemical engineering first

Fed-batch fermentation, CSTRs, oxygen transfer, SOPs and batch records. Never toy datasets for their own sake.

3

Verify before you trust

Every model, policy and agent is checked against balances, constraints and tests before anyone relies on it.

4

Idea to deployment

Each course ends with a shareable app or service, not a notebook that only runs on one laptop.

5

Any AI platform

Taught with Claude by default; the method transfers to ChatGPT, Gemini, GitHub Copilot or Cursor.

6

Built from research

The examples come from published RL control of bioprocesses and from twins deployed in industry and a startup.

Your instructor

Sakib Ferdous, Ph.D.

Sakib is a chemical engineer and AI researcher who builds digital twins and learning controllers for bioprocesses, and carries them from equations to deployed tools. He holds a Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering from Iowa State University and is the first graduate of Iowa State’s M.Sc. in Artificial Intelligence. His doctoral work built Game of Cells, a cell-culture digital twin for training control policies, and reinforcement-learning strategies for CAR T-cell expansion and fed-batch enzyme production, published in Biotechnology and Bioengineering and the AIChE Journal.

He then took the same ideas into production. At Corteva Agriscience he deployed a company-wide decision pipeline on Docker and Kubernetes. At Craft BioSolutions, a startup founded on this research, he co-owns the physics-based bioreactor twin and its device-to-cloud stack. He learned AI for chemical engineering the hard way, back when the code was the wall. These courses are built so the next engineer does not have to.
