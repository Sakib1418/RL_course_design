Reinforcement Learning for Chemical Engineers: Vignettes and Vibe Coding

Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) is a branch of machine learning in which an autonomous agent learns optimal decision-making through iterative interaction with an environment, guided by reward signals that shape policy improvement over time. Unlike supervised learning, RL is inherently suited to sequential decision problems—making it a natural fit for chemical engineering challenges such as process control, fed-batch fermentation optimization, reaction pathway selection, and resource scheduling. Despite this alignment, RL has seen limited adoption in mainstream chemical engineering curricula and practice. The historical barrier lies at the intersection of unfamiliarity with modern software ecosystems, the steep learning curve of RL libraries (e.g., OpenAI Gym, Stable Baselines3, Ray RLlib), and a disciplinary culture that has been slow to integrate computational AI methods at the undergraduate and early graduate levels. The result is a widening gap between the potential of RL and its accessibility to most chemical engineers.
This work presents a pedagogical framework built on two complementary approaches: conceptual vignettes and AI-assisted “vibe coding.” We begin by introducing RL through an interactive bioreactor optimization game, in which students observe the agent-environment-reward loop in action without writing a single line of code. This scaffolded exposure builds intuition for core RL components—state space, action space, reward function, and policy—within a bioprocess context directly relevant to chemical engineering practice.

Building on this foundation, we demonstrate how large language model (LLM)-assisted code generation—colloquially termed vibe coding—dramatically lowers the entry barrier to applied RL. Students articulate a process problem in natural language and iteratively co-author functional RL environments and training pipelines with an AI coding assistant, bypassing the need for deep software engineering expertise. Two case studies are presented: (1) a vibe-coded game environment in which an agent learns dynamic strategy through trial and reward, and (2) a vibe-coded bioprocess simulation in which an RL agent learns to maximize product titer under substrate and dissolved oxygen constraints—closely mirroring real industrial fermentation scenarios.

We further introduce a prototype platform that accepts a natural language process description as input and generates a deployable, code-minimal RL training environment. This tool enables rapid prototyping of RL-based controllers for practitioners with limited programming backgrounds, and represents a concrete step toward democratizing RL across chemical engineering sub-disciplines.

Our findings suggest that pairing structured conceptual scaffolding with LLM-assisted coding can reduce the time from concept to working RL prototype by an order of magnitude. These approaches are broadly applicable across ChE education and industrial practice, and offer a scalable model for building AI literacy in the next generation of chemical engineers.
